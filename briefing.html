The customer & the ask

4 seats · 60 minutes · 5 use cases · complete as many as you can. Diagnose what the customer actually needs, place the right Cisco platforms on the network, and earn the right to the next meeting.

The customer

A distributed enterprise: a corporate HQ and on-premises data centre, a fleet of remote branches on SD-WAN, a growing remote and hybrid workforce — some of them now running AI agents — and an application estate spread across on-prem, AWS and Azure. They've grown by acquisition, they're mid-flight into public cloud, and their security controls haven't kept pace with their footprint.

The ask

They are not asking you to rip anything out. They're asking you to bring Zero Trust to an environment that already exists — to segment it, see it, and keep enforcing policy no matter where the user, the device, or the workload happens to be.

Welcome to the design clinic. In today's threat landscape, static network boundaries are no longer sufficient. To achieve a true Zero Trust posture we shift from reactive security to an iterative, dynamic approach. Your group will design a SASE environment to satisfy the five use cases using the ZT Access and Agentic Identity controls discussed earlier. By integrating SD-WAN, Cisco ISE, policy controls, telemetry and any tool in the Cisco toolbox, you'll move beyond simple connectivity to an intelligent, digitally resilient network.

Step 5 of 6

Segmentation methodology

A short primer on how modern segmentation works, so the whole team shares the vocabulary. This is reference material — not the answer. Apply it to each use case.

Why VLANs / subnets alone struggle

  • Segmentation tied to IP/VLAN means topology changes ripple everywhere — complex and tedious.
  • Access rules become long IP ACLs that are hard to read, audit and maintain.
  • Every new device type needs new subnets and rules; staff can't keep up.

Identity-based (group) segmentation

  • Classify each user/device into a group at the point of access (authentication + profiling).
  • Carry the group as a tag with the traffic, independent of IP / VLAN / topology.
  • Write policy as group-to-group (who may talk to whom), enforced across the existing network.

The building blocks

  • Authenticate & profile every endpoint (802.1X, MAB, web auth) and learn what it is.
  • Posture — check endpoint health (AV, OS, patches) and remediate before granting full access.
  • Tag & enforce — assign a group tag; switches / routers / firewalls enforce group policy inline.
  • Share context — feed identity/group to firewalls, data center, SD-WAN and threat tools for consistent policy and rapid containment.

How to approach each use case

  • 1 · Map pain → product. For every customer pain point, name the product/feature that fixes it.
  • 2 · Say how & why. For each product, one line on how it works and why it's the right fit (differentiators welcome).
  • 3 · Read the task & parameters. Note the specific groups, access rules and constraints.
  • 4 · Propose a diagram. Draw the topology with your solution on it, then submit for scoring.
