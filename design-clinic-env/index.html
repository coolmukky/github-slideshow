Why this is the "before" picture

One shared identity for three agents. The support, reconciliation, and fraud-triage agents all authenticate as svc-halcyon and are then trusted to act broadly. There is no way to tell which agent did what.

Follow the red lines: every agent collapses into one account, and that account has standing access to the ledger database, the mail server, partner-bank APIs, and SaaS tooling.

Flat network, long-lived credentials, no per-call check. Everything sits on one VLAN. Tokens don't expire. No policy checkpoint authorizes an agent→resource call, and nothing writes an identity-bound audit record.

A single compromised agent could read, move, or alter customer funds undetected. That's the blast radius your target-state design has to shrink.

How to use this in the clinic

  1. Walk the estate. Click each node, open its application, and note the identity it runs as and what it can reach.
  2. Mark the trust. Every red dashed line is standing, over-broad access. List the ones that must become per-agent, just-in-time, and checkpoint-verified.
  3. Redesign. On your canvas, give each agent its own identity, put a policy checkpoint on every agent→resource call, segment the network, and add an identity-bound audit log.
  4. Defend it. Re-run the walk against your target state — can you now answer "which agent did what," and can one compromised agent still roam?
