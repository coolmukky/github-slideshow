A Zero Trust Fable

The Kingdom of Cleartext

How a walled realm learned to trust no one — and everyone learned to prove themselves. A storyboard that decodes an old fable into Cisco Zero Trust architecture.

I
Chapter I

The Crumbling Wall

Why a wall that trusts everyone within is no wall at all.

Perimeter
The Story

For a hundred years the realm slept behind a single great wall and a black moat. Within that ring no traveler was questioned — to stand inside the wall was to be trusted, and that was the whole of the law.

In Cisco Terms

The classic perimeter model: a hardened edge — firewall and VPN — around a soft, flat interior. Whatever was already inside the network was implicitly trusted, and east–west traffic went largely uninspected.

PerimeterImplicit trustFlat network
One wall, one moat
The Story

But the great wall had a hundred small gates, and every gate had a key, and every key had been copied and copied again. A single borrowed key opened not one door but the whole realm behind it.

In Cisco Terms

Shared credentials, broad VPN tunnels, and flat VLANs mean one stolen key unlocks everything. Password reuse and over-broad access turn a single secret into keys to the entire kingdom.

Shared credentialsVPN sprawlOver-broad access
A hundred gates, a thousand keys
The Story

At dusk a thief slipped through a servant’s gate. No one barred the way, for he was already inside; unquestioned, he walked the length of the realm straight to the glittering treasury.

In Cisco Terms

This is lateral movement. An attacker who phishes one account or breaches one host pivots freely across a flat, trusting network — from a low-value foothold to the crown-jewel assets.

Lateral movementPhishingBlast radius
The thief who was never stopped
The Story

The Sovereign stood upon the cracked rampart and saw the truth at last: a wall that trusts all who stand within it is no wall at all. The old law would have to be unwritten.

In Cisco Terms

The founding premise of Zero Trust: never trust, always verify. Remove trust granted purely by network location. “Inside” can no longer mean “safe.”

Zero TrustNever trustAssume breach
The Sovereign’s reckoning
II
Chapter II

Every Traveler Named

The name became the new gate — and a name alone was never enough.

Identity
The Story

The Sovereign summoned the Herald and set a new decree upon every gate: none shall pass unnamed. Each traveler must first declare who they are and prove the claim before a single bolt is drawn.

In Cisco Terms

Strong identity becomes the new perimeter. Every user, device, and workload must authenticate before access. Cisco ISE authenticates network access with 802.1X and RADIUS at the point of entry.

Identity-firstAuthenticationCisco ISE
No one passes unnamed
The Story

A name alone could be borrowed, so the Herald demanded a second sign — a sigil the true bearer alone could show. A stolen name without its sigil opened nothing at all.

In Cisco Terms

Multi-factor authentication (Cisco Duo): something you know plus something you have. A password by itself — even a correct one — is no longer enough to pass the gate.

MFACisco DuoPhishing-resistant
A name and a second sign
The Story

Even a proven name did not suffice. The Herald looked to each traveler’s bearing and health — their wounds, their fevers — and turned away any who might carry rot within the walls.

In Cisco Terms

Device trust and posture: Duo Device Trust and ISE posture checks verify patch level, disk encryption, and management state. An identity on a sick or unmanaged device is granted less — or nothing.

Device postureEndpoint trustHealth check
Proven, and proven whole
The Story

And names were not checked once but at every threshold, for a traveler honest at dawn may be turned by dusk. Trust, the Herald wrote, is a question asked again and again.

In Cisco Terms

Continuous authentication. Trust is re-evaluated at every access request, not granted once at login and left standing. Sessions are short-lived and constantly re-verified.

Continuous authRe-verificationSession trust
Named again at every door
III
Chapter III

Seals and Sigils

A right the baker did not need was a right the baker was never given.

Least Privilege
The Story

The realm was carved into many small wards, each ringed by its own low wall, and each traveler’s sigil named only the doors they were meant to open — no more.

In Cisco Terms

Segmentation and least privilege. Cisco TrustSec assigns Security Group Tags (SGTs) that travel with identity, carving one flat network into many enforced segments.

SegmentationTrustSecSGT
The realm, carved into wards
The Story

The baker’s sigil opened the granary and the mill, but never the armory. A right the baker did not need was a right the baker was never given.

In Cisco Terms

Least-privilege, role-based policy. A Security Group ACL lets “Baker” reach “Granary” and denies “Armory” — enforced by role, not by IP address or where the traveler happens to stand.

Least privilegeSGACLRole-based
The baker at the armory door
The Story

When fire took the middle ward, the inner gates held fast, and the flames found no road into the wards beside it. What burned, burned alone.

In Cisco Terms

Micro- and macro-segmentation contain blast radius. East–west traffic between segments is inspected and filtered (Secure Firewall, SD-Access), so a breach in one zone cannot spill into the next.

Blast radiusEast–westContainment
A fire that could not spread
The Story

The same door judged each traveler differently, reading the role written in their sigil — the same threshold an open way for one and a barred one for the next.

In Cisco Terms

Group-based policy: a single destination enforces different rules per source group. Policy follows identity, not topology — the answer depends on who is asking, not the wire they arrive by.

Group policyIdentity-basedContext-aware
One door, two verdicts
IV
Chapter IV

The Watchful Eye

Not to punish, but to see — an eye that never closes.

Continuous Verification
The Story

The Sovereign raised the Sentinel, whose single eye never closed, to watch every road and every ward of the realm at once — not to punish, but to see.

In Cisco Terms

Continuous monitoring and telemetry. Cisco Secure Network Analytics (Stealthwatch) watches all traffic and baselines what normal looks like across the whole environment.

MonitoringTelemetrySecure Network Analytics
The eye that never closes
The Story

The Cartographer drew a living map that redrew itself as travelers moved, so that no journey through the realm went unseen or unrecorded.

In Cisco Terms

Full visibility. NetFlow and telemetry build a live map of who talks to what, while Cisco Umbrella inspects and logs DNS as the very first hop of every connection.

VisibilityNetFlowCisco Umbrella
The map that never sleeps
The Story

When a courier’s path bent strangely — from the granary road toward the armory it had no cause to seek — the Sentinel marked it in the same breath.

In Cisco Terms

Behavioral anomaly detection. A host that suddenly scans or reaches a segment it never touches raises an alert — Encrypted Traffic Analytics flags it from patterns alone, without decrypting the payload.

Anomaly detectionBehavioral baselineETA
The path that bent wrong
The Story

What the eye saw, the gates obeyed. A traveler turned suspect had their sigil dimmed at once, their doors quietly closing before any harm was done.

In Cisco Terms

Automated response. XDR / SecureX orchestration revokes access through ISE — Rapid Threat Containment — the moment risk rises, quarantining a host without waiting for a human hand.

Automated responseRapid Threat ContainmentXDR
Seen, and answered
V
Chapter V

The Living Charter

Trust was no longer a wall to stand behind, but a tide.

Adaptive Policy
The Story

The old laws were carved in stone and never changed. The new Charter was written on living vellum, made to rewrite itself the moment the realm learned something new.

In Cisco Terms

Policy as code and adaptive policy. Cisco SD-Access and Catalyst Center express intent centrally and push it everywhere automatically — no more hand-editing rules gate by gate.

Policy as codeSD-AccessIntent-based
Laws that rewrite themselves
The Story

When a single gate learned the face of a new deceiver at dawn, the Charter taught every other gate before nightfall. No lesson was learned twice.

In Cisco Terms

Coordinated, automated response. A verdict from one control — Umbrella, Secure Firewall, or Duo — updates policy fleet-wide through XDR, so a threat seen once is blocked everywhere.

Coordinated defenseThreat intelXDR
One gate teaches all
The Story

Trust was no longer a wall to stand behind but a tide — rising for the traveler who proved themselves true, falling for the one whose conduct turned strange.

In Cisco Terms

Risk-based, continuous trust scoring. Each access decision weighs live signals — identity, posture, behavior, threat intelligence — so trust rises and falls per request rather than being fixed at login.

Risk-based accessContinuous trustAdaptive
Trust became a tide
The Story

And so the kingdom let its great wall fall to points of light, for it no longer needed one. The whole realm had become watchful, and trust was a thing earned newly each day.

In Cisco Terms

The mature Zero Trust end state: no implicit trust anywhere. Identity, context, and continuous verification replace the perimeter entirely — the network defends itself, everywhere, all the time.

Zero TrustNo implicit trustDefense in depth
A realm that needed no wall
